# Twins: One Feels Like a Gift, the Other Like a Puzzle...

![Cover](parenting-twins-cover.png)

> "I've always been suspicious of 'fragmented reading' — and for good reason."

He's a strange little creature.

Forgets his schedule. Every morning: *"What class do I have today?"*

Forgets where his uniform went. Drops it, and it vanishes into thin air.

Losing things is baseline. Zoning out is default.
*"Wait… what was I supposed to do?"*

Can't sit still. Can't stand still. Just… bounces.

Homework posture? Lying down. Prone. Squatting. Once, I found him under the desk.
— just refuses to sit normally.

I used to think he was being lazy.
Turns out, he's just busy.

Five minutes of writing.
Three water breaks. Two bathroom trips. Four pencil sharpenings.
Page still blank.

But show him something he loves? He locks in.

Ants on the sidewalk.
He crouches for forty minutes.
Names them. Draws a family tree. Knows exactly who's in charge.

I ask him to grab something from his room.
Five minutes later, he's in the living room, snapping LEGO bricks together.
*"Weren't you going to your room?"*
He blinks. *"Oh. Right."*
Like a browser that just finally loaded.

Yet, he's relentlessly happy.

I've realized success isn't about achievement. It's about ease.
Many adults spend half a life chasing peace.
He's already there. Doing nothing. Just being.

Mornings are a slow parade of humming.
He makes up songs while brushing his teeth. Breakfast is a concert.
Takes marching tunes and turns them into animal-sound remixes. The rhythm is uncannily accurate. You almost forget the original melody.

His emotional shock absorption is terrifyingly strong.
Yell? Scold? Sigh? Roll eyes?
He just absorbs it. Smiles. Bounces back.
Like a punching bag made of memory foam.
Sometimes I wonder if he's just watching me lose my mind for entertainment.

But there's one thing that cracks him open.
Not anger. Not punishment.
Silence.

When I stop talking, he unravels.
*"Mom? What's wrong?"*
*"Mom, please say something."*
*"Mom, can you just smile?"*
His voice drops to a whisper: *"Mom… please don't ignore me."*

He's not numb. He's just selectively sensitive.
Noise bounces off him. But love? That goes straight in.

Sometimes I think he's not disobedient.
He's just operating on a frequency the rest of us can't tune into.
It's chaotic. Jumpy. Rule-defying.
But it's also alive.
Alive enough to get scolded and start humming again three seconds later.

A friend told me: *"You've got a rare one. Auditory learner. Rhythm-driven. Interest-powered. He's not slow. The standard mold just doesn't fit him."*

I was skeptical.
So I tried something different. Fast-paced, highly animated phonics videos. A teacher who talks like he's doing stand-up comedy.

Guess what happened.
He sat still.
Actually sat.
Repeating sounds, laughing at the jokes, genuinely absorbing it.
He wasn't resisting learning. He was waiting for the right channel.

![Inner Illustration](parenting-twins-inner.png)

Some kids don't need to be fixed.
They just need someone who can speak their language.

It took me a while to accept it:
Some kids aren't here to make life easy.
They're here to crack it open.

He exhausts me. But he also showed me that humans are allowed to live this loudly, this messily, this joyfully.
I wanted to smooth out his edges.
Turns out, he was sanding down mine.
He's not the child I imagined.
But he's the one I no longer want to put in a box.

And if you think dealing with this little hurricane means my life is set…
Think again.

He's only one half of the equation.
I have twins.
And the other one?
Is a completely different story.
